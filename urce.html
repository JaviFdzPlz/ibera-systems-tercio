TERCIO / L0 / HUMAN-WORN PASSIVE SIGNATURE CONDITIONING

URCECondition the signature at source.

URCE is the TERCIO capability focused on human-worn passive signature conditioning. Its role is to explore how material architecture, spacing and surface geometry can reduce the stability and recognisability of thermal cues without relying on power, emissions or network connectivity.

01 / OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

Camouflage is weakest when the human signature remains coherent.

Visual concealment does not necessarily prevent a person from presenting a stable thermal contour during movement, pauses or equipment contact. Once the human form becomes sufficiently coherent, observation and classification may become easier.

URCE addresses this problem at source by exploring passive textile and wearable structures intended to break up thermal continuity, reduce persistent contour cues and complement position-level concealment.

02 / CAPABILITY LOGIC

Disrupt coherence.
Preserve wearability.

URCE is not an invisibility concept and does not depend on active cooling. The design logic is based on passive conditioning through material selection, spacing, surface topology and asymmetric zoning.

The engineering challenge is deliberately balanced: signature disruption must be developed together with comfort, mobility, durability, compatibility with existing equipment and practical manufacture.

03 / CANDIDATE MVP PATHWAY

INITIAL DEVELOPMENT PACKAGE

URCE Textile Coupon Programme

A comparative materials programme using small textile coupons and wearable sub-assemblies rather than a complete garment. Candidate combinations of base fabrics, spacing structures and surface geometries are observed against a conventional reference under controlled LWIR conditions.

Development focus: apparent contour behaviour, thermal gradients, material stability, moisture response, abrasion and compatibility with realistic garment construction.
FOLLOW-ON PATHWAY

Wearable Integration Article

Promising coupon concepts may progress into a limited wearable article focused on selected body zones and equipment interfaces. The objective is to study the balance between thermal conditioning and human factors before any full-garment configuration is considered.

Development focus: comfort, mobility, heat burden, snag risk, equipment interaction, repairability and repeatable production.

04 / REPRESENTATIVE TEST LOGIC

Compare materials.
Then test the wearer-system.

The URCE route begins with controlled comparison of materials and geometries before introducing garment complexity. Environmental conditions, viewing geometry and reference articles should be recorded so results remain traceable.

Once a viable physical effect is identified, wearer trials should evaluate signature behaviour together with comfort, mobility and equipment compatibility. These are treated as one integrated development question rather than separate disciplines.

Material behaviour

LWIR appearance, thermal transients, moisture response, ageing and repeatability.

Human factors

Comfort, heat burden, mobility, fit and compatibility with carried equipment.

Industrial readiness

Fabrication route, assembly repeatability, repair, quality control and scalable sourcing.
