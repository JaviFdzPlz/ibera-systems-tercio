Working logic

MILLARES starts from the threat and the protection required. We examine different ways of responding, challenge what we think we know and engineer the response that makes sense for the problem — without assuming the product in advance.

As the threat changes, we reassess the protection and the engineering direction.

01 / Why

Warfare does not stand still.

Threats can change faster than protection can be conceived, engineered and fielded. New attack methods, technologies and tactics can alter what people, platforms, assets and infrastructure need protection from — sometimes before the previous answer has fully arrived.

A technically sound response can lose relevance when the assumptions behind it stop matching the battlefield.

Threats evolve

New technologies and tactics create new attack paths and new combinations.

Conditions shift

Terrain, mission, signatures, connectivity and operating constraints change what protection needs to do.

Engineering commits

Real systems take time and resources. Decisions matter more as they become harder to reverse.

Protection has to keep pace with the threat.

02 / How we work

Start with what can defeat the protection.

We begin with the threat, what needs protecting and the conditions in which that protection has to work. Only then do we decide what should be engineered.

That may point towards a new system, an adaptation, an integration, a service or another engineering response. The answer can be familiar or unconventional; the point is that it follows from the problem rather than being chosen before the problem is understood.

From inquiry to engineering

Analysis is not the destination. Engineering is.

MILLARES uses the knowledge and tools that help answer the problem: operational experience, engineering, research, modelling, simulation, experimentation, computation and AI-assisted analysis where useful.

Their purpose is practical — to understand the threat, compare real alternatives, find weak assumptions and decide what is worth engineering. We explore alternatives to improve the engineering choice, not to avoid making one.

R

Why repeat?

The threat can change after the decision.

Choosing an engineering direction does not make the assumptions behind it permanently true. If the threat, operating conditions or available evidence change materially, we return to the question that matters: does the protection still fit the problem?

If it does, the commitment stands. If it does not, the response needs to be reconsidered.

03 / What we protect

People. Platforms. Assets. Infrastructure.

MILLARES looks at protection across different parts of the modern battlespace rather than treating protection as one product problem.

01

People

Personnel and other people exposed to direct, indirect or evolving threats across operational environments.

02

Platforms

Manned and unmanned operational platforms across land, air, maritime and other domains, with protection needs shaped by role, exposure and mission.

03

Assets

Mission-critical equipment, capabilities, nodes, materiel and other resources whose loss or degradation can affect operations.

04

Infrastructure

Fixed, semi-fixed or deployable infrastructure that sustains operations, logistics, communications, command and other critical functions.

These are protection domains, not product divisions. The engineering response is determined by the problem in each case.

04 / What we are developing

Protective engineering for contested, adaptive threats.

MILLARES is focused on a small number of bounded problems: keeping warning usable when evidence is degraded or contradictory; reducing exposure and observability; making deception measurable rather than assumed; and connecting heterogeneous warning and response components through bounded, human-authorised interfaces.

We are not presenting a fixed product architecture. The first outputs we intend to build and test are concrete engineering objects — interface contracts, adapters, local warning modules, replay and test harnesses, and measurement protocols.

Counter-UAS is a priority application and test bed, not a claim to own a complete stack.

Early-stage R&D. Candidate capability directions and experiments; not fielded products and not operational-effectiveness claims.

01 / Trusted warning

Keep warning usable when the picture is uncertain.

We are developing and testing warning functions for incomplete, contradictory, stale or manipulated observations, with uncertainty and provenance exposed rather than hidden.

Objects: data/interface adapters, local warning modules, degraded-input replay and test harnesses.

Partners: sensing/data, communications, HMI, operational users and test organisations.

02 / Observability

Reduce what the adversary can reliably see and follow.

We are exploring bounded ways to reduce exposure and observability through position, timing, emission discipline and partner-provided passive measures, measured against a defined baseline.

Objects: exposure-management tools, protective-state software, integration interfaces and measurement data products.

Partners: observability/signature specialists, platform teams, instrumentation and operational users.

03 / Deception

Make deception something we can test, not assume.

We are exploring controlled ambiguity and deception together with the instrumentation and measurement needed to determine whether they actually change observation, classification, tracking or resource allocation.

Objects: deception-control logic, partner-integrated elements, replay tools, measurement protocols and effect-assessment records.

Partners: deception/signature specialists, modelling, instrumentation, threat representation and operational validation.

04 / Interoperability

Connect existing warning and response components without pretending to own the whole stack.

We are defining bounded interfaces and decision hand-offs between heterogeneous sensing, warning, decision and existing response components, while keeping explicit human authority over any weapon-release decision.

Objects: interface contracts, adapter modules, local decision-support functions, conformance/replay harnesses and human-authority traces.

Partners: C-UAS and other component developers, C2/integration, sensing/warning, safety/verification and Ukrainian operational/test routes.

Buildable objects

Interface contracts, adapters, local warning and decision modules, replay/test harnesses and measurement protocols.

Evidence objects

Defined baselines, degraded cases, trial records, explicit uncertainty, falsifiers and negative or no-effect results.

Convergence rule

A direction narrows, changes or stops when access, evidence or redundancy shows that another answer is better.

Protective continuity

Test beyond the first successful event.

When a bounded capability becomes real enough to test, we challenge it against repeated demand, degraded communications, node or link loss, common-mode failure, constrained resources, configuration change and recovery.

Continuity is a stress condition on the work above, not a claim that MILLARES already operates a mature orchestration platform.

Bring a real protection problem, component or test route. We will define the smallest experiment that can show whether working together adds value.

05 / Evidence & research

What we know. What we are testing. What remains open.

The strength of a claim should match the evidence behind it. Models and analysis can help compare assumptions, mechanisms and alternatives; claims about real-world performance need evidence from the relevant system and context.

MILLARES is developing its research and engineering practice. The approach itself is not presented as proof of better protection, and no general claim of superior performance is made.

Observe

Let new evidence change the question.

Operational observations and empirical evidence can force us to reconsider what the protection problem actually is, not just tune a preferred answer.

Challenge

Use models as tools, not as reality.

Analysis, simulation and AI-assisted work can expose weak assumptions, compare alternatives and generate questions. Agreement between models is not a substitute for evidence of physical effectiveness.

Test

Design work that can change the engineering decision.

Experiments and trials matter most when their outcomes can discriminate between meaningful alternatives, reject a weak direction or justify commitment.

Questions we keep open

  • Which assumptions are most likely to fail as a threat evolves?
  • What evidence is strong enough to narrow an engineering choice?
  • When does continued exploration stop adding enough value to justify its time and cost?
  • What change in the threat would justify reopening a previous decision?

06 / About

A defence research and engineering initiative built around a moving problem.

MILLARES exists to explore how protective responses can be conceived, challenged and engineered when the threat, evidence and operating conditions cannot be treated as fixed.

We draw on established science, engineering disciplines, domain knowledge, computation and experimentation as the problem requires. The method is not the product. The destination is an accountable engineering response when the case justifies one.

The exact form of that response is intentionally not fixed in advance.

07 / Work with us

Bring a real problem. Build the smallest useful experiment.

We are looking for Ukrainian and European partners who can bring current operational problems, existing technologies worth integrating or challenging, specialist measurement capability, test access or field feedback.

Current validate-first collaboration tracks include degraded warning/interoperability and measurable observability/deception. Which one leads will be decided by operational relevance, partner access, non-redundancy and the shortest path to falsifiable evidence.

Sensors, effectors, specialist technologies, platforms and test infrastructure may be partner-provided. We do not need to own every component to contribute useful engineering — and we will not invent a role when an existing solution already closes the gap.
