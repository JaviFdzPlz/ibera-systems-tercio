01 / What we are developing

Protective engineering for contested, adaptive threats.

MILLARES is focused on four bounded problems: keeping warning usable when evidence is degraded or contradictory; reducing the reliability of detection, classification and tracking; measuring whether controlled ambiguity or deception changes an adversary's picture; and connecting heterogeneous warning and response components through bounded, human-authorised interfaces.

We are not presenting a fixed product architecture. Candidate first outputs are concrete engineering and evidence objects — interface contracts, adapters, local warning or decision functions, replay and conformance harnesses, baselines, measurement protocols and trial records.

Counter-UAS is a priority application and test bed, not a claim to own a complete stack.

Current stage: early-stage R&D, public capability framing and bounded experiment design. The objects below are candidate development outputs, not completed fielded products, and no operational-effectiveness claim is made.

01 / Trusted warning

Keep warning usable when the picture is uncertain.

We are developing and testing warning functions for incomplete, contradictory, stale or manipulated observations, with uncertainty and provenance exposed rather than hidden.

Candidate objects: data/interface adapters, local warning functions, degraded-input replay and test harnesses.

Partner inputs: sensing/data, communications, HMI, operational problems and test access.

02 / Observability

Reduce the reliability of detection, classification and tracking.

We are exploring bounded exposure-management logic and measurement around position, timing and emissions. Specialist passive, material or signature technologies may be partner-provided rather than assumed to be owned by MILLARES.

Candidate objects: exposure-management tools, protective-state software, integration interfaces and baseline/delta measurement records.

Partner inputs: observability/signature expertise, platforms, instrumentation and operational users.

03 / Deception

Measure whether ambiguity changes the adversary's picture.

We are developing controlled trial and effect-assessment methods for ambiguity and deception. The mechanism remains open and may be partner-provided; the engineering question is whether a defined intervention measurably changes observation, classification, tracking or resource allocation.

Candidate objects: trial designs, integration controls, replay tools, measurement protocols and effect-assessment records.

Partner inputs: deception/signature technologies, modelling, instrumentation, threat representation and operational validation.

04 / Interoperability

Connect warning and response components without pretending to own the whole stack.

We are defining bounded interfaces and decision hand-offs between heterogeneous sensing, warning, decision and existing response components.

Human authority: any weapon-release decision remains explicitly human-authorised and outside an automated hand-off.

Candidate objects: interface contracts, adapter modules, local decision-support functions, conformance/replay harnesses and authority traces.

Partner inputs: C-UAS and other component developers, C2/integration, sensing/warning, safety/verification and operational/test routes.

Buildable objects

Bounded interfaces, adapters, local functions and harnesses that can be reviewed, exercised and changed.

Evidence objects

Baselines, degraded cases, trial records, explicit uncertainty, falsifiers and negative or no-effect results.

Decision rule

A direction narrows, changes or stops when evidence, access or redundancy shows that another answer is better.

For either track, the first experiment should fix the problem, partner inputs, object under test, baseline and degraded conditions, evidence to capture, and the rule for narrowing, changing or stopping the direction.

Protective continuity

Test beyond the first successful event.

When a bounded capability becomes real enough to test, we challenge it against repeated demand, degraded communications, node or link loss, common-mode failure, constrained resources, configuration change and recovery.

Continuity is a stress condition on the work above, not a claim that MILLARES already operates a mature orchestration platform.

Bring a real protection problem, component or test route. We will define the smallest experiment that can show whether working together adds non-redundant value.

02 / What we protect

People. Platforms. Assets. Infrastructure.

MILLARES looks at protection across different parts of the modern battlespace rather than treating protection as one product problem.

01

People

Personnel and other people exposed to direct, indirect or evolving threats across operational environments.

02

Platforms

Manned and unmanned operational platforms across land, air, maritime and other domains, with protection needs shaped by role, exposure and mission.

03

Assets

Mission-critical equipment, capabilities, nodes, materiel and other resources whose loss or degradation can affect operations.

04

Infrastructure

Fixed, semi-fixed or deployable infrastructure that sustains operations, logistics, communications, command and other critical functions.

These are protection domains, not product divisions. The engineering response is determined by the problem in each case.

03 / How we work

Start with what can defeat the protection.

Threats can change faster than protection can be conceived, engineered and fielded. A technically sound response can lose relevance when the assumptions behind it stop matching the battlefield.

We begin with the threat, what needs protecting and the conditions in which that protection has to work. Only then do we decide what should be engineered.

That may point towards a new system, an adaptation, an integration, a service or another engineering response. The answer follows from the problem rather than being chosen before the problem is understood.

From inquiry to engineering

Analysis is not the destination. Engineering is.

MILLARES uses the knowledge and tools that help answer the problem: operational experience, engineering, research, modelling, simulation, experimentation, computation and AI-assisted analysis where useful.

Their purpose is practical — understand the threat, compare real alternatives, expose weak assumptions and decide what is worth building, integrating or testing.

R

Why repeat?

The threat can change after the decision.

Choosing an engineering direction does not make the assumptions behind it permanently true. If the threat, operating conditions or available evidence change materially, we return to the question that matters: does the protection still fit the problem?

If it does, the commitment stands. If it does not, the response needs to be reconsidered.

04 / Evidence & research

What we know. What we are testing. What remains open.

The strength of a claim should match the evidence behind it. Models and analysis can help compare assumptions, mechanisms and alternatives; claims about real-world performance need evidence from the relevant system and context.

MILLARES is developing its research and engineering practice. The approach itself is not presented as proof of better protection, and no general claim of superior performance is made.

Observe

Let new evidence change the question.

Operational observations and empirical evidence can force us to reconsider what the protection problem actually is, not just tune a preferred answer.

Challenge

Use models as tools, not as reality.

Analysis, simulation and AI-assisted work can expose weak assumptions, compare alternatives and generate questions. Agreement between models is not a substitute for evidence of physical effectiveness.

Test

Design work that can change the engineering decision.

Experiments and trials matter most when their outcomes can discriminate between meaningful alternatives, reject a weak direction or justify commitment.

Questions we keep open

  • Which assumptions are most likely to fail as a threat evolves?
  • What evidence is strong enough to narrow an engineering choice?
  • When does continued exploration stop adding enough value to justify its time and cost?
  • What change in the threat would justify reopening a previous decision?

05 / About

A defence research and engineering initiative built around a moving problem.

MILLARES exists to explore how protective responses can be conceived, challenged and engineered when the threat, evidence and operating conditions cannot be treated as fixed.

We draw on established science, engineering disciplines, domain knowledge, computation and experimentation as the problem requires. The method is not the product. The destination is an accountable engineering response when the case justifies one.

The long-term form is not fixed in advance. Each bounded experiment, however, should fix its object under test, assumptions and decision criteria before the evidence is interpreted.

06 / Work with us

Bring a real problem. Build the smallest useful experiment.

We are looking for Ukrainian and European partners who can bring current operational problems, existing technologies worth integrating or challenging, specialist measurement capability, test access or field feedback.

Current validate-first tracks are degraded warning/interoperability and measurable observability/deception. No lead is preselected: operational relevance, credible access, non-redundancy and the shortest path to evidence able to change the decision will determine what comes first.

We do not need to own every component to contribute useful engineering — and we will not invent a role when an existing solution already closes the gap.
