TERCIO / L3 / DISTRIBUTED GEOMETRY EXTENSION

ALANOMove the sensing geometry forward.

ALANO is the TERCIO capability focused on attritable forward and remote mission nodes that extend local sensing and cueing around obstacles, dead ground and masked approaches. Its role is geometry, persistence and relay—not kinetic interception.

01 / OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

Terrain hides threats.
It also hides sensors.

Dismounted teams operate in terrain where vegetation, structures, trenches, folds in the ground and short-range masking can limit line of sight and compress warning. Increasing sensor power or transmission strength does not automatically solve a geometry problem.

ALANO addresses this by moving selected sensing or relay functions forward, laterally or into otherwise inaccessible positions through low-cost, recoverable or expendable nodes designed to remain useful under attrition.

02 / CAPABILITY LOGIC

Geometry over power.
Distribution over concentration.

ALANO extends the architecture without becoming a mandatory dependency. Each node should provide bounded local value, share event-level cues when useful and degrade gracefully when links or neighbouring nodes are lost.

The public baseline keeps ALANO focused on sensing, relay and geometry extension. Effectors, autonomous engagement and hard-kill functions remain outside the current scope.

03 / CANDIDATE MVP PATHWAY

CANDIDATE ROADMAP PACKAGE

ALANO Geometry Node

A compact forward node intended to test whether displaced sensing or relay geometry can provide useful incremental awareness in one agreed deployment scenario. The initial configuration should remain deliberately narrow and reuse proven interfaces wherever possible.

Development parameters include node placement, local autonomy, cue format, communications discipline, retrieval concept, signature burden and attrition tolerance.
FUTURE GROWTH PATH

Mission-Configured Node Family

Later work may examine a shared core with mission-specific sensing, relay or deployment kits. Commonality should simplify training, maintenance and supply while preserving the ability to tailor geometry to terrain and threat.

Any family architecture is matured only after the first node, interfaces and deployment concept have been demonstrated.

04 / REPRESENTATIVE TEST LOGIC

Prove the geometry gain.
Then prove the network.

The first ALANO programme should compare a baseline local-sensor configuration with one or more displaced-node placements under representative masking. The test question is not whether a node can transmit, but whether its placement creates useful, timely and reliable additional information.

Local node behaviour, cue quality and failure response should be established before wider cooperative configurations are introduced.

Geometry value

Incremental cueing or coverage created by node placement in agreed terrain and masking conditions.

Link discipline

Event delivery, emissions burden, degraded operation and recovery after link loss.

Attrition tolerance

Operational effect of node loss, replacement burden, serviceability and retrieval options.
