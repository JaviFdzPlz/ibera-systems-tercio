TERCIO / SENSE & RESPOND / STAND-OFF INTERCEPTION & DENIAL

CIDA partner-led interception and denial concept.

CID is the TERCIO concept for partner-led, stand-off interception and denial of an already-detected threat. It is presented here strictly as an architecture, interfaces and development-posture concept: CID is not built, tested, fielded or authorised for autonomous or explosive weapon release, and no interception effectiveness is claimed.

01 / OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

Warning and deception do not remove every threat.

Even where ATALA generates warning and ARDID creates ambiguity, some threats will still complete approach and commitment. TERCIO treats the interval immediately before impact as a distinct, bounded problem rather than an extension of earlier sensing or deception functions.

CID exists to define, at architecture level, how a stand-off interception or denial response to an already-detected and already-committed threat could be structured, authorised and controlled, without asserting that such a response currently exists.

02 / CAPABILITY LOGIC & BOUNDARY

Own the function.
Borrow the platform.

CID owns the interception/denial function, its authorisation logic and its assessment. Where a physical response node is required, CID may task a compatible ALANO-hosted mission-kit module; ALANO provides platform, power, geometry and communications only.

Boundary rule: ALANO hosts. CID owns the interception/denial function, its control logic and its evidence, whether or not it is physically carried on an ALANO node.

No autonomous weapon release, explosive interceptor payload or lethal anti-personnel effect is described, claimed or authorised on this page. Any future physical concept would require separate owner authorisation, a defined human-control model and dedicated safety, legal and evidence review before any public claim beyond architecture level.

03 / DEVELOPMENT POSTURE

Earlier-stage than a committed pathway.

CID is earlier-stage than GADIR-TB, which remains the only currently committed TERCIO Phase A demonstrator. CID has no committed public demonstrator, no defined test programme and no performance figure at this time.

The near-term objective is to identify a qualified stand-off interception/denial partner able to help define a bounded architecture study, interface specification and authorisation/control model, before any physical development or test question is proposed.

Define the architecture before defining a device.

ÍBERA Systems is seeking a qualified interception/denial partner to help scope a bounded CID architecture study: authorisation model, human-control boundary, interfaces to ATALA/ARDID/ALANO and applicable legal, safety and export-control constraints.
